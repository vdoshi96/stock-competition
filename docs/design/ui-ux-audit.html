Three biggest moves

Most of the value comes from three structural changes. The remaining findings are polish that the same rebuild absorbs.

1One standings table, not three

The same nine rows render as Leaderboard, Live Stock Rows, and Current Value. Merge them into one table with Today, vs SPY, and rank movement columns. Put secondary price data behind a row expander.

2Answer “what changed today?”

People revisit a friendly contest to see movement. The snapshot already holds daily history, so the client can derive day change and rank movement with no new API calls.

3Never lose the data you already have

A failed refresh replaces the whole dashboard with an error panel. Keep the last snapshot visible, show an inline alert, and add busy, freshness, and auto-refresh behavior.

UX-01
High

The same nine rows render three times

Evidence
Leaderboard (Dashboard.tsx:689-731), Live Stock Rows (:733-759), and Current Value bars (:806-817) each list every participant with rank, ticker, return, and balance.
Impact
At 390 px the three lists become 27 stacked cards, about 5,000 px of scrolling. On desktop, the eye must match rows across two side-by-side tables.
Recommendation
One Standings table: Rank (with movement), Participant, Return, Today, vs SPY, Balance (diverging bar). Shares, Dec 31 price, latest price, and quote time move into an expandable detail row.
Plan: Task 4 · Tests: standings suite
UX-02
High

A failed refresh wipes a valid dashboard

Evidence
The catch in fetchSnapshot sets state to "error" (Dashboard.tsx:415-418). The render guard state !== "ready" (:611) then swaps the whole page for the error panel, even though snapshot still holds good data.
Impact
One flaky Yahoo response, or a phone moving between networks, blanks the page the participant was reading.
Recommendation
Separate initial-load failure (full error panel) from refresh failure (inline alert above the standings: “Couldn't refresh prices. Showing data from …” with Try again). Keep rendering the last snapshot.
Plan: Task 5 · Tests: header and refresh suite
UX-03
High

No signal for what changed since the last close

Evidence
The UI shows only year-to-date return. snapshot.histories already includes every daily close, and group_avg_history identifies the previous complete trading day.
Impact
“Did I move up?” and “What did my stock do today?” are the main reasons to reopen a friendly contest. Today the dashboard can't answer either question.
Recommendation
Add a Today column (price change since the previous close) and a rank-movement marker (▲2, ▼1, —) with screen-reader text. Derive both client-side.

UX-04
High

Market comparison is buried and approximate

Evidence
“Group vs Market” is a separate hand-drawn SVG at the bottom (Dashboard.tsx:193-298) with no hover values and rotated 7 px labels on mobile. Per-pick comparison with SPY requires mental subtraction between two cards.
Impact
“Am I beating the market?” is the contest's core question, and the page makes participants compute it.
Recommendation
Add a vs SPY column (percentage points), a Beating SPY tile (“3 of 9”), and fold the benchmark view into the main chart as the “Group vs market” preset.
Plan: Tasks 4, 6, and 7
UX-05
Medium

Developer diagnostics occupy primary space

Evidence
The always-visible status banner shows “Quotes refreshed from batched Yahoo data”, API calls 13 / 24, quote batches, and fetch time (Dashboard.tsx:656-686). The footer repeats the provider pipeline.
Recommendation
Show a data notice only when quotes or histories are degraded. Move the diagnostics into a collapsed “Data details” disclosure in the footer.
Plan: Tasks 5 and 6 · Tests: summary suite
UX-06
Medium

Identical timestamps repeat twelve times

Evidence
“Sep 22, 2026, 07:59 PM ET (post-market)” renders on every holdings row and benchmark card (Dashboard.tsx:316, :651).
Recommendation
Show one relative freshness label in the header (“Updated 3 min ago”, absolute time on hover). Flag individual rows only when their quote isn't live, with a Delayed badge. Keep the full quote label in the row details.
Plan: Tasks 4 and 5
UX-07
Medium

The comparison chart is hard to drive and colors collide

Evidence
Fourteen checkboxes plus a duplicate Chart.js legend. COLORS has eight entries for nine users (Dashboard.tsx:22, :451), so Nari (SOFI) and Singh (MU) share blue. Colors follow rank, so they change when ranks change. MU's +284% flattens every other line with no range control. Two chart technologies use different axis styles.
Recommendation
One Chart.js chart with presets (Top 3 + SPY, Everyone, Group vs market, Custom), ranges (1W, 1M, 3M, YTD), colored chips that double as the legend, fixed per-ticker colors, and an index tooltip sorted by value.

UX-08
Medium

Mobile header is tall and hides freshness

Evidence
Below 920 px the sticky header stacks and full-width buttons wrap, reaching about 128 px (15% of an 844 px viewport). Below 620 px .updatedAt is display: none (dashboard.module.css:811). The leader card's decorative ::after stroke crosses the balance on mobile. GitHub competes with Refresh as a header button.
Recommendation
A 60 px single-row header: logo, title, freshness line, icon-only Refresh (40 × 40). Move GitHub to the footer. Remove the decorative stroke.
Plan: Tasks 5 and 9 · Tests: layout and accessibility
UX-09
Medium

Summary cards are unbalanced

Evidence
At 1440 px the leader sentence wraps to five lines in a narrow card. Five equal-weight metric cards follow. At 390 px six full-width cards take about 700 px before the rankings start.
Recommendation
A leader card (name, ticker, return, balance, vs SPY) plus four compact tiles: Group average, Ex-crypto average, S&P 500 (with VT and VTI), and Beating SPY. Use a 2 × 2 tile grid on mobile.
Plan: Task 6
UX-10
Medium

Refresh gives no feedback, and open tabs go stale

Evidence
The Refresh button has no busy or disabled state. The inFlight guard silently drops repeat clicks (Dashboard.tsx:378). After the first load the client never polls, although the server cache refreshes every 5 minutes.
Recommendation
Busy state (“Refreshing…”, disabled, aria-busy), a relative freshness label that ticks every 30 seconds, and a 5-minute auto-refresh while the tab is visible, plus an immediate refresh when a stale tab becomes visible.
Plan: Task 5
UX-11
Medium

Loading state doesn't resemble the page

Evidence
Four generic shimmer bars (Dashboard.tsx:328-356) precede a very different layout, and the timeout copy says “Still warming up backend cache”.
Recommendation
A skeleton that mirrors progress, summary, and nine rows, with plain status text: “Building today's snapshot. Retrying in 15 seconds.”
Plan: Task 5
UX-12
Medium

Accessibility gaps

Evidence
Light --positive #009e73 is 3.42:1 on white, below the 4.5:1 AA minimum for the 0.86 rem return text. No :focus-visible styles, no reduced-motion handling for the shimmer and hover lift, no table captions, no skip link. VTI's #0f2e5c line is 1.33:1 against the dark surface.
Recommendation
New tokens (--positive: #007a5a, 5.34:1), a global focus ring, prefers-reduced-motion overrides, a caption, a skip link, and a light VTI color (#38bdf8).

UX-13
Low

Jargon and unexplained rules

Evidence
“Live Stock Rows”, “Filtered average”, rank hints (“First”, “Second”), and an unexplained “Crypto-adjacent” label. The page never says every pick starts with $1,000 at the Dec 31, 2025 close.
Recommendation
Rename to Standings and Ex-crypto average. Add a “How scoring works” disclosure and a tooltip on the Crypto-adjacent chip.
Plan: Tasks 4 and 6
UX-14
Low

No competition context and no share path

Evidence
There is no indication of how far into the year the contest is. Standings reach the group chat only as cropped screenshots.
Recommendation
A progress strip (“Day 265 of 365 · 100 days left”) and a Copy standings button that writes a plain-text summary to the clipboard.
Plan: Tasks 6 and 8 · Tests: sharing
UX-15
Low

Balance bars misrepresent losses

Evidence
Bars scale to the largest balance with an 8% floor (Dashboard.tsx:163). A −34% loss still draws a bar growing right, with no $1,000 reference.
Recommendation
Diverging bars centered on 0% return: gains grow right in green, losses grow left in red, scaled to the largest absolute return.

Redesigned workflows

The four journeys participants repeat most, before and after. Red steps mark friction; green steps are new capabilities.

1. Glance: who's winning and what moved today?

Before ~1,000 px of scrolling on mobile

Open page→Scroll past 128 px header and 6 cards→Read rank and YTD→Daily movement unavailable

After First viewport

Open page→Leader card and 2 × 2 tiles→Standings with ▲▼ and Today
2. How is my pick doing against the market?

Before 6 steps, mental math

Find row in Leaderboard→Find SPY card and subtract→Scroll to chart→Uncheck 4 defaults→Check own pick and SPY→Hover: nearest point only

After 2 steps

Read vs SPY column→Tap name, then Show on chart→Chart scrolls in with pick added; index tooltip
3. Share the standings in the group chat

Before Screenshot, crop, send

Screenshot→Crop two tables→Send image

After 1 tap

Copy standings→Paste: ranked text, averages, SPY, link
4. Refresh and recover

Before Silent, then destructive

Tap Refresh→No feedback→On failure the page is replaced by an error

After Visible and safe

Tap Refresh→“Refreshing…”→On failure: inline alert, data kept, Try again→Auto-refresh every 5 min
